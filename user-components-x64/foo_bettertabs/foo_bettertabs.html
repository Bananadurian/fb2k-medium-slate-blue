Better Tabs#

Better Tabs is a tab container for foobar2000 v2. Every panel (Columns UI) or element (Default UI) you put in it gets a tab in a strip along one edge, and a click on a tab shows its panel.

This page covers all of Better Tabs, and title formatting and searching in foobar2000 too, so you can look everything up in one place.

Better TabsSetting it up, the strip, and every setting in Configure.Title formattingEvery field and function, with examples. For tab titles and conditions, and anywhere else in foobar2000.SearchingThe query syntax of foobar2000’s searches and autoplaylists.

Getting started#

Adding the container

  • Columns UI: in Preferences › Display › Columns UI › Layout, add Splitters › Better Tabs and add panels under it. Each panel is a tab.
  • Default UI: turn on View › Layout › Enable layout editing mode, right-click where the container should go and pick Replace UI Element... › Containers › Better Tabs. It starts with one empty tab: click it to pick an element. For more tabs, right-click the strip and pick Add new tab....

Opening the settings

Right-click the strip and pick Configure.... Columns UI also has it on the Layout page, and Default UI’s layout editing mode as Configure Better Tabs....

  • The settings belong to each container, so two containers can look and behave differently.
  • Every change shows on the strip right away, and Cancel undoes them all.
  • The dialog doesn’t block foobar2000: you can hover and click the tabs while you try settings.
  • Hover over a setting’s label to see what it does and its range.
  • Help (or F1) opens this page at the section for the page you are on.

The strip menu

Right-click the strip. What you get depends on where you click: on a tab, the items for that tab; on empty strip space, the tab list and the rest.

  • The tab list: every tab in the strip, the shown one ticked. Pick one to show it.
  • The panel’s own items (Columns UI): the menu of the panel you right-clicked.
  • Rename...: a title for the tab. See Tab titles.
  • Hide tab: takes the tab out of the strip. The last tab can’t be hidden.
  • Move left and Move right (Move up and Move down on a side strip).
  • Pin tab: Pin to the left, Pin to the right (the top and the bottom on a side strip) and Unpin.
  • Show hidden tab: the tabs you hid. Pick one to bring it back. Only there while a tab is hidden.
  • Appearance: the main look settings at hand: Strip position (with Rotate titles on side strips), Active tab (with its Fill strength), Accent colour, Strip background, Tab width (with the alignment, the chevron at the start and shorter titles), Show the strip, Show every tab as a chip and Show a pin on pinned tabs.
  • Configure...: all the settings.

While Configure is open, the items that change tabs or settings are greyed out: the dialog’s OK or Cancel would undo them.

Several tabs at once

Ctrl+click tabs to select them, or Shift+click for a range. Right-click one of them for Hide 2 tabs (with the number you selected), Pin tabs and Clear selection. Drag one of them and the others come along. The last tab of the strip always stays, so you can’t hide them all.

Tabs that don’t fit

When the strip is too short for all tabs, the ones that don’t fit leave it and a chevron appears at its end (or its start, Strip page). Click the chevron for a list of all tabs. Pinned tabs stay in view. With Shorten titles before showing the chevron the longest titles get shorter first.

Default UI layout editing

In layout editing mode the strip’s menu is Default UI’s: Add new tab..., Paste as new tab and, for the tab you clicked, Replace tab's element..., Copy tab's element, Rename tab... and Remove tab, then Configure Better Tabs.... Right-click an element inside the container for Rename tab..., Remove tab and Configure Better Tabs....

Settings#

Configure... has ten pages. Each section below is one page, with every setting and its default. The buttons at the bottom:

  • Defaults puts the settings of every page except Tabs back to their defaults. Cancel still undoes it.
  • OK keeps the changes, Cancel undoes them all.
  • Help (or F1) opens this page at the section for the page you are on.

Colours are hex RRGGBB, such as 3EA6FF; click the swatch beside one to pick it instead.

Titles and conditions#

A tab’s title can be plain text or title formatting, and two of a tab’s settings, Show only if and Only if, are title formatting too. The Title formatting part of this page has every field and function; this section is about how Better Tabs uses them. The examples were run in Better Tabs itself, with the sample tracks of that part.

Tab titles#

Set a title with Rename... in the strip menu or on the Tabs page. An empty title is the panel’s own name. Tick Use title formatting to make the title a pattern; Help beside it opens this section.

While a track plays, the fields come from the playing track, as in foobar2000’s status bar. While nothing plays there is no track: every field is empty, and a title that comes out empty shows the panel’s name instead.

%artist%→The Beatlesplaying Here Comes the Sun
%artist%→the panel’s namestopped
Lyrics[: %title%]→Lyrics: Here Comes the Sunplaying Here Comes the Sun
Lyrics[: %title%]→Lyricsstopped
$if(%isplaying%,Now playing,Playlist)→Now playingplaying Here Comes the Sun
$if(%isplaying%,Now playing,Playlist)→Playliststopped
$if(%ispaused%,Paused,Playing)→Pausedpaused Here Comes the Sun

While a track plays, a title that comes out empty stays empty. Give such a title something to fall back on:

[%conductor%]→an empty titleplaying Here Comes the Sun
$if2(%conductor%,Conductor)→Conductorplaying Here Comes the Sun

Titles change when playback starts, moves to another track, pauses or stops, and when the playing track’s tags or its dynamic info (a stream’s title, say) change. They don’t follow the playback time: %playback_time% shows the time of the last change.

%playback_time%→0:16playing Here Comes the Sun

What tab titles can’t show

  • Only the first line: a title with $crlf() ends there.
  • No colours: $rgb() and the like give colour codes for playlists, which a tab shows as stray characters such as 0000FF.
  • No playlist fields: %list_index%, %queue_index% and the like give ?, because a tab title has no playlist.
%list_index%→?playing Here Comes the Sun
%queue_index%→?playing Here Comes the Sun

Show only if#

A tab with Show only if (Tabs page) is in the strip only while that title formatting gives some text; spaces don’t count. It runs for the playing track, or for no track while stopped, so the same rules apply as for titles. Empty means always.

$if(%isplaying%,1)→1in the stripplaying Here Comes the Sun
$if(%isplaying%,1)→nothingnot in the stripstopped
$if(%isplaying%,,1)→1in the stripstopped
[$info(video_codec)]→nothingnot in the stripplaying Here Comes the Sun

$if(%isplaying%,1) shows a tab during playback, $if(%isplaying%,,1) only when stopped, and [$info(video_codec)] only while a video plays (a music track gives nothing, as above).

  • It is checked when playback starts, moves to another track, pauses or stops, and when the playing track’s tags change. Not as the playback time runs.
  • A tab it hides keeps its place and comes back by itself, so Show hidden tab doesn’t list it.
  • If it hides the shown tab, its neighbour is shown instead. If no tab is left, the container stays empty until one comes back.

Tabs that follow playback#

Two settings on the Tabs page make a tab come up by itself:

  • Switch to it: When playback starts (play, next, previous, a double-clicked track) or On every new track (the next track of a playlist too, and the one foobar2000 resumes as it starts).
  • When playback stops.

Only if is title formatting for the new track, or for the track that stopped: the switch happens only if it gives some text.

When several tabs want the switch, one with Show only if or Only if set wins over one without. Among equals the shown tab stays if it is one of them, otherwise the first one in the strip wins. Tabs that aren’t in the strip take no part.

For example

Video, Lyrics and Slideshow all switch On every new track. Video has Show only if [$info(video_codec)], Lyrics has Only if $if($stricmp(%genre%,Instrumental),,1). A video brings up Video, other songs Lyrics, instrumentals Slideshow. A video with words keeps Lyrics if that is the tab shown: both fit.

Add Playlist and Properties, both When playback stops, Properties with Only if $if(%genre%,,1): a track without a genre brings up Properties as it stops, any other track Playlist.

Good to know#

Pinned tabs
Pinned tabs keep to the start or the end of the strip (the top or the bottom of a side strip) and stay in view when the other tabs overflow into the chevron. Pin them from the strip menu or on the Tabs page. They move only among themselves: dragging, the menu and Move up/Move down stop at the edge of their group, and in Columns UI’s Layout page a panel moved out of its group goes back to it. A pinned tab without an icon shows a pin unless Show a pin on pinned tabs is off.
Icons
Enter a code point (such as E8D6) or paste a character. Copy Segoe Fluent Icons from Character Map, and emoji from Win+..
Size in Columns UI
The container is at least as large as its largest panel minimum and can grow to the largest panel maximum. A panel with a smaller maximum keeps its own size at the top left instead of shrinking the whole container (Columns UI’s Tab stack uses the smallest maximum).
Missing elements
A panel whose component is gone shows (missing element) and keeps its settings, so it comes back with its component. Removing the last tab leaves an empty one.
Columns UI
The strip menu also has the panel’s own items, and FCL export and import keep the container’s settings.
Auto-hide
The strip stays while its menu is open, while you drag a tab and while it has the keyboard focus.
Transparency
Only shows something when your layout draws a background behind the strip, such as a Columns UI theme. The auto-hide strip shown over the panel stays solid. When the container changes its background (a new cover, say), it has to repaint the panels inside it; the strip picks up the new background with that repaint.
Text colours
The Colours page sets the titles of the other tabs and of the active (and selected) tabs; the Hover page sets a hovered tab’s title, separately for the active tab. Colours you pick are used as they are. Brightens on the active tab lightens towards white, which does nothing to white text.
Performance log
Preferences › Advanced › Display › Better Tabs: log performance to the console.

Title formatting#

Title formatting turns a track’s tags and technical information into text. foobar2000 uses it for playlist columns, the status bar, the window title, file names and more, and many components take it too. In Better Tabs it makes tab titles and the conditions of the Tabs page.

Every example here was run in foobar2000 v2.26.1 on the eight sample tracks below. What follows the arrow is what foobar2000 gave, for Here Comes the Sun unless another track is named. In the results, · is a space where it matters, ⇥ a tab and ↵ a line break.

How a pattern works#

A pattern is text with fields and functions in it. Plain text comes out as it is; the rest is replaced.

%name% is a field
It stands for a value of the track, mostly a tag. A field the track doesn’t have gives ?.
%artist% - %title%→The Beatles - Here Comes the Sun
%title% - %conductor%→Here Comes the Sun - ?
$name(...) is a function
It works on what you give it in the parentheses, separated by commas.
$upper(%title%)→HERE COMES THE SUN
$left(%album%,5)→Abbey
[...] shows only with a value
The part in brackets disappears when the fields in it have no value.
%title%[ - %album%]→Here Comes the Sun - Abbey Road
%title%[ - %album%]→The Beatles - Hey JudeThe Beatles - Hey Jude
'...' is text as it is
Quotes keep characters that would mean something else, such as %, $, [, ], (, ) and ,. Two quotes give one.
%title% '('live')'→Here Comes the Sun (live)
it''s %title%→it's Here Comes the Sun
'50%' $upper(%title%)→50% HERE COMES THE SUN
Line breaks and comments
Line breaks are left out, so a long pattern can be spread over lines. A line that starts with // is a comment.
// artist first %artist% - %title%→The Beatles - Here Comes the Sun

True and false#

$if(), [...] and other functions that choose don’t look at the text, they look at whether it is true. True roughly means “a field had a value”:

  • A field is true when the track has it.
    • $if(%composer%,yes,no)→yes
    $if(%conductor%,yes,no)→no
  • Plain text is never true, not even 1.
    • $if(abc,yes,no)→no
    $if(1,yes,no)→no
  • A function is true when a field it was given is true, whatever text comes out.
    • $if($len(%title%),yes,no)→yes
    $if($len(abc),yes,no)→no
    $if($cut(%title%,0),yes,no)→yes
  • A test, such as $strcmp() or $greater(), is true when the test passes. Some give the text 1, others no text at all.
    • $strcmp(a,a)→1
    $greater(3,2)→nothing
    $if($greater(3,2),yes,no)→yes
  • $if(), $if2() and $select() are true when what they chose is true.
    • $if($if2(%conductor%,none),yes,no)→no
    $if($if2(%composer%,none),yes,no)→yes
  • $get() is true when the variable is set, even to plain text.
    • $puts(x,abc)$if($get(x),yes,no)→yes
  • [...] shows when anything inside it is true.
    • [%conductor% ]%title%→Here Comes the Sun
    [$strcmp(a,a) x]→1 x
    [x $len(abc)]→nothing

Fields#

Most fields read tags. Some fall back to other tags when theirs is missing, and some give technical information or come from where the pattern runs.

Technical information#

%codec%

The format, such as FLAC or MP3; the file extension if the format has no name.

%codec%→FLAC
%codec%→MP3The Beatles - Hey Jude
%codec_profile%

More about the encoding, when there is something to say.

%codec_profile%→CBRThe Beatles - Hey Jude
%codec_long%

The format’s long name; otherwise the codec and the profile.

Mistakes and surprises#

  • A misspelt field gives ?, a misspelt function an error text.
    • %titel%→?
    $uper(%title%)→[UNKNOWN FUNCTION]
  • A function with the wrong number of arguments gives nothing; $if(), $if2(), $ifequal(), $ifgreater() and $iflonger() give an error text. A comma in an argument has to be quoted.
    • $if(1,2,3,4)→[INVALID $IF SYNTAX]
    $upper(one, two)→nothing
    $upper('one, two')→ONE, TWO
  • A parenthesis outside a function ends the output. Quote it.
    • %title% (live)→Here·Comes·the·Sun·
    %title% '('live')'→Here Comes the Sun (live)
  • Inside [...] or a function, // has to be quoted. Unquoted, it makes them disappear, with everything after them.
    • %album artist% - %title%[ // %track artist%]→Daft Punk - Get LuckyGet Lucky
    %album artist% - %title%[ '//' %track artist%]→Daft Punk - Get Lucky // Daft Punk, Pharrell Williams, Nile RodgersGet Lucky
  • Spaces count, also after a comma.
    • [$if(%composer%, by %composer%)]→·by·George·Harrison
  • A missing closing parenthesis or quote ends the output there.
    • %title% $upper(%album%→Here·Comes·the·Sun·
    %title% 'and more→Here·Comes·the·Sun·
  • Number functions and tests drop decimals, and take text as 0.
    • $ifequal(3,3.9,equal,not equal)→equal
    $ifgreater(abc,0,greater,not greater)→not greater
  • $strcmp(), $strstr() and $replace() care about case; the fields’ names don’t.
    • %TITLE%→Here Comes the Sun

Searching#

A query picks tracks. Library › Search and autoplaylists take one, and so do many components that filter tracks. A query can be a few words, or field comparisons joined with AND, OR and NOT.

The examples ran on the same eight sample tracks. What follows the arrow is what foobar2000 found.

Simple search#

Words without keywords find the tracks that have every word somewhere: in any tag or in the file’s path. Part of a word is enough, case doesn’t matter, and letters with accents match the plain ones.

beatles→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together; The Beatles - Hey Jude The Beatles is the artist of the first two; the untagged Hey Jude file has the word in its file name.
beatles abbey→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together Every word has to be found: the Hey Jude file name has Beatles but not Abbey.
floyd wall→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2 The words can be in different tags: Floyd is in the artist, Wall in the album.
pharr→Get Lucky Part of a word is enough: Pharrell Williams is one of the artists of Get Lucky.
harrison→Here Comes the Sun Every tag counts, not only artist, title and album: George Harrison is its composer.
beyonce→Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It) Accents don’t matter: the artist is Beyoncé.
unsorted→The Beatles - Hey Jude The path counts too: the Hey Jude file is in a folder called Unsorted.

Searching a tag#

field HAS words
The tag contains every one of the words, as parts of its text.
title HAS come→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together Part of a word counts, so Comes matches too.
artist HAS floyd→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2
genre HAS rock→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together; Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2 Progressive Rock has the word too.
field IS text
The tag is exactly the text, whatever the case. With several values (two artists, say), one of them is enough.
artist IS pink floyd→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2
artist IS pharrell williams→Get Lucky One of the three artists of Get Lucky.
genre IS rock→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together Rock is one of the two genres of Here Comes the Sun; Progressive Rock isn’t exactly Rock.
Wildcards
In IS, * stands for any number of characters and ? for one.
artist IS the*→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together
title IS ?om*→Come Together; Comfortably Numb
* HAS words
Any tag or the path: like a simple search, but it can be combined with the rest.
* HAS punk→Get Lucky
* HAS unsorted→The Beatles - Hey Jude

A field name can have spaces; double quotes around it are optional.

"album artist" IS pink floyd→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2
album artist IS pink floyd→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2

Numbers#

field GREATER N, field LESS N and field EQUAL N compare whole numbers.

tracknumber LESS 3→Come Together; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It); Thursday Afternoon
%length_seconds% GREATER 300→Comfortably Numb; Get Lucky; Thursday Afternoon; The Beatles - Hey Jude
date GREATER 2000→Get Lucky; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It)
%rating% EQUAL 5→Here Comes the Sun

Present or missing#

field PRESENT finds the tracks that have the tag, field MISSING those that don’t.

composer PRESENT→Here Comes the Sun; Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2
genre MISSING→The Beatles - Hey Jude
"album artist" PRESENT→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2; Get Lucky

Dates and times#

AFTER, BEFORE, SINCE and DURING compare times. A time is YYYY, YYYY-MM or YYYY-MM-DD; after a full date can come a space and hh, hh:mm or hh:mm:ss. The field needs percent signs: %date%, %last_modified%.

%field% AFTER time, %field% BEFORE time
After or before the whole of that time: AFTER 1979 starts with 1980. A track without the field counts as before.
%date% AFTER 1979→Get Lucky; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It); Thursday Afternoon
%date% BEFORE 1979→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together; The Beatles - Hey Jude
%field% SINCE time
That time or later.
%date% SINCE 1979→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2; Get Lucky; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It); Thursday Afternoon
%field% DURING time
Within that time.
%date% DURING 1969→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together
%date% DURING 1979-11→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2
%last_modified% DURING 2024-05→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together
%field% DURING LAST N DAYS
Within the last N seconds, minutes, hours, days or weeks: SECONDS, MINUTES, HOURS, DAYS, WEEKS. For one, DURING LAST DAY and so on. There are no months or years.
%last_modified% DURING LAST 2 WEEKS finds the files changed in the last two weeks.

Without the percent signs the query is an error:

date AFTER 1979→Invalid date/time format

Combining#

A AND B
Both.
genre IS rock AND tracknumber LESS 3→Come Together
A OR B
Either.
artist IS pink floyd OR artist IS brian eno→Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2; Thursday Afternoon
NOT A
Not A.
NOT genre HAS rock→Get Lucky; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It); Thursday Afternoon; The Beatles - Hey Jude
ALL
Every track.
ALL→all eight tracks

AND goes before OR. Parentheses change that:

artist IS pink floyd OR artist IS the beatles AND genre IS pop→Here Comes the Sun; Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2
(artist IS pink floyd OR artist IS the beatles) AND genre IS pop→Here Comes the Sun

Title formatting in a query#

A field with %, $ or # in it is title formatting rather than a tag name. That reaches everything title formatting knows: technical information, fallbacks, Playback Statistics, functions.

%codec% IS mp3→The Beatles - Hey Jude
codec IS mp3→no tracks
%title% IS the beatles - hey jude→The Beatles - Hey Jude
title IS the beatles - hey jude→no tracks
%album artist% IS the beatles→Here Comes the Sun; Come Together
"album artist" IS the beatles→no tracks

A pattern with a function in it has to be in double quotes, or the query is an error.

$upper(%genre%) IS AMBIENT→Invalid filter expression
"$upper(%genre%)" IS AMBIENT→Thursday Afternoon
"$len(%title%)" GREATER 20→Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It); The Beatles - Hey Jude

PRESENT and MISSING with title formatting ask whether it is true, not whether it gives text:

"$stricmp(%genre%,ambient)" PRESENT→Thursday Afternoon
"[%composer%]" PRESENT→Here Comes the Sun; Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2
"$if(%composer%,1)" PRESENT→no tracks

Title formatting makes a search slower on a large library, and IS sees all values of a tag as one text:

%artist% IS pharrell williams→no tracks
%artist% IS daft punk, pharrell williams, nile rodgers→Get Lucky

Sorting#

SORT BY pattern at the end sorts what was found by a title formatting pattern; SORT ASCENDING BY is the same, SORT DESCENDING BY sorts the other way.

genre HAS rock SORT BY %title%→Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2; Come Together; Comfortably Numb; Here Comes the Sun
genre HAS rock SORT DESCENDING BY %length_seconds%→Comfortably Numb; Come Together; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2; Here Comes the Sun
ALL SORT BY %date%→The Beatles - Hey Jude; Here Comes the Sun; Come Together; Comfortably Numb; Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2; Thursday Afternoon; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It); Get Lucky

A pattern with a function goes in double quotes here too. ALL SORT BY "$rand()" shuffles.

ALL SORT BY $len(%title%)→Invalid filter expression
ALL SORT BY "$len(%title%)"→Get Lucky; Come Together; Comfortably Numb; Here Comes the Sun; Thursday Afternoon; The Beatles - Hey Jude; Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It); Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2

Mistakes and surprises#

  • Keywords are uppercase. In lowercase they are words to look for.
    • genre is rock→no tracks
  • Without percent signs a field is a tag: technical information and Playback Statistics need them.
    • rating EQUAL 5→no tracks
  • foobar2000 rejects a query it can’t read. A lone parenthesis is one cause; in double quotes it is fine.
    • title HAS (put→Invalid filter expression
    title HAS "(put"→Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It)
    genre IS→Invalid filter expression
  • A simple search looks in the path too, so a word of a folder name finds everything in that folder.
    • music→all eight tracks
